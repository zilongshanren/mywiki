The mechanics for maintaining attention and stimulating in-game spending in popular video games increasingly resemble those used in classic slot machines and other specialized entertainment platforms. The only difference is that in traditional gambling, the user is well aware that they take financial risks for the potential of monetary winnings, whereas in video games, excitement is skillfully disguised as the acquisition of exclusive virtual rewards. The blurring of these boundaries is increasingly causing controversy among gamers, psychologists, and government regulators around the world.


## Loot Boxes and Gacha Mechanics: A Virtual Wheel of Fortune

Loot boxes and so-called gacha mechanics have become the most addictive elements that have become a crossover between video games and gambling. Players of such popular titles, like Fortnite, purchase a virtual container, capsule, or crystal with real money, containing a random in-game item, from a useless spray to an incredibly rare and beautiful skin for their favorite character.

The process of opening such a container replicates the psychology of a slot machine down to the smallest detail. Developers implement complex audiovisual effects: bright flashes of light, a simmering drumbeat, and slow animation before the item appears on the screen. All this is done with one goal in mind: to trigger a powerful dopamine rush in the player. The brain reacts not so much to the item itself as to the anticipation of the reward.

The use of a random number generator when distributing loot makes the process completely unpredictable, forcing users to purchase containers again and again in the hopes of «knocking out» the desired item with a 0.1% drop rate.

## The Psychology of Rewards: Variable Reinforcement

The defining aspect that has made modern games with live service mechanics so special is their classic behavioral psychology research, particularly the experiments of Burrhus Skinner. He demonstrated that so-called «variable reinforcement», where rewards are delivered on an unpredictable schedule, forms the strongest and most persistent habits.

If a player knew that the sword or character they wanted would drop from exactly the tenth chest, the process would become a boring routine. But since the reward can drop on the first try or the hundredth, the player is constantly in a state of emotional tension. This phenomenon has been studied for decades in the classic gambling industry, and today it has become the foundation of the economy of most free-to-play multiplayer hits, from mobile RPGs to competitive shooters for PC and consoles.

## The Illusion of Virtual Currencies And The Loss of Control Over Spending

Another powerful tool that blurs the boundaries between gaming and gambling is the conversion of real money (fiat) into in-game premium currency. Players purchase not the items themselves, but rather virtual «V-bucks», «diamonds», «gems», or «gold coins».

This intermediate step serves a crucial psychological function: it breaks the connection between spending and real money in the user’s mind. Spending 2000 «crystals» is psychologically much easier than withdrawing 20 euros from a bank card.

A similar mechanic has historically been used in casinos, where players exchanged cash for plastic chips. Service-based games use inconsistent currency pack sizes – skin costs 800 coins, but you can only purchase packs of 500 or 1000 coins. As a result, the player always has a certain balance, which motivates him to make another deposit so that these virtual funds do not burn out in vain.

## Skin Betting And The Gray Market For Virtual Assets

The moment when video games started to use mechanics that are similar to the gambling industry is when in-game items acquired a real market value. In games like Counter-Strike 2 or Dota 2, skins can be freely transferred to other users through the built-in marketplace or third-party services. Due to artificial scarcity, the price of some rare virtual knives or gloves can reach tens of thousands of dollars.

This has given rise to a colossal gray market for so-called «skin betting». Third-party roulettes and platforms have emerged where players bet not with money, but with their in-game items. The virtual slot machine has become a fully-fledged token with a fiat equivalent. This has led to underage players, who are unable to access traditional platforms due to age restrictions, now having the opportunity to gamble using their own in-game equipment.

## Key Elements That Connect Service Games With Gambling

It is always the most difficult task for developers to make sure that the audience keeps growing even after the game was released to the public years ago. There are a few mechanics that video games have got from gambling and end up using to retain attention among players:

- Near Miss Effect. The in-game roulette animation often shows the arrow literally missing the most valuable prize by a millimeter and landing on a cheaper item. This tricks the brain into believing that success was incredibly close, stimulating the next attempt.
- Artificial Scarcity and FOMO. Stores in games-as-a-service are updated daily or weekly. Unique skins are sold for a limited time, triggering a fear of missing out and encouraging impulse purchases.
- Daily Login Bonuses. A mechanic that encourages users to open the app every day, creating a strong habit of interacting with the interface.
- Hidden Probabilities. A «guarantor» system where the odds of landing a rare item increase after a series of losing streaks, enticing the player to continue spending money to «spin» the roulette wheel to a 100% chance of success.
- VIP Systems and Loyalty Levels. The more money a user spends in the game, the higher their status, which grants exclusive avatar frames, titles, and small cashbacks in virtual currency.

These marketing and psychological techniques create a powerful ecosystem in which the user constantly feels a pressing need to make new transactions. Understanding these mechanisms is the first and most important step toward developing a healthy attitude toward modern digital entertainment.

## The Global Community’s Reaction And The Evolution of Monetization

The aggressive introduction of gambling mechanics into products accessible to children and adolescents has not gone unnoticed. Government regulators in many countries have begun actively intervening in the activities of gaming corporations. In Belgium and the Netherlands, loot boxes were officially recognized as a form of unlicensed gambling and completely banned in video games. In other jurisdictions, developers were required to publicly disclose the exact drop probabilities of all items in chests.

Under pressure from the public and legislators, the industry has gradually begun to transform, shifting its focus from loot boxes to the Battle Pass system. In this model, players see all the rewards they will receive in advance, but unlocking them requires dozens of hours of gameplay. Although the Battle Pass is considered a fairer and more transparent monetization model, it still actively exploits FOMO, as the pass is strictly limited in time, and players are often forced to purchase additional levels with real money to claim the final rewards.

Modern live service games are highly complex software products, designed at the intersection of game design, behavioral psychology, and big data. They offer magnificent virtual worlds, but they also require a high degree of digital literacy and self-control from the consumer, lest innocent entertainment devolve into an endless pursuit of virtual brilliance.
