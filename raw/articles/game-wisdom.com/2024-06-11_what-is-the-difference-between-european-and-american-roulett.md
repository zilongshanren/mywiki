The game of roulette was invented by French mathematician [Blaise Pascal](https://iep.utm.edu/pascal-b/) as he attempted to devise a perpetual motion machine. While his search was ultimately fruitless, it did give rise to early forms of the table game.

## The Wheel – European Roulette

Early European roulette wheels had two spaces for the bank – a 0 and a 00. But in the mid-19th Century, a new wheel design was introduced in Germany, which only featured the 0. This version, with its single bank space, would go on to be widely adopted in European gambling halls and casinos and is what we know today as the European roulette wheel.

## The Wheel – American Roulette

In contrast, the American roulette wheel uses the more traditional design with a 0 and a 00. Originally, American roulette also had a third bank space, emblazoned with an eagle, but this is rarely seen on modern tables, if ever.

## The House Edge – European Roulette

The European roulette wheel has a total of 37 number spaces. There are 18 red numbers, 18 black numbers, and a green 0 space. Although players can bet on the 0 space, it is this green slot that gives the house the advantage. Any bets placed on colors, odds or evens, or sets of numbers, won’t pay out if the ball lands on the 0. And this is when the house makes money. With just one bank space, it means that the bank has a 1 in 37 chance of winning at European roulette, giving a house edge of 2.7%.

## The House Edge – American Roulette

American roulette has the same red and black numbers, and the green 0. It also has a green 00. Because there are two bank spaces, the house has a 2 in 38 chance of winning, equivalent to a house edge of 5.26%.
