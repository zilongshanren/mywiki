**What is Card Counting, and is it Legal?**

Card counting is a strategy used in blackjack and other card games to determine whether the next hand will likely give the player or the dealer a probable advantage. It involves keeping track of the high and low cards that have been dealt with to anticipate the odds of certain cards coming up. The principle is that high cards (10s, face cards, and aces) are generally better for the player, while low cards (2 through 6) are better for the dealer. By maintaining a running count and sometimes adjusting for the number of decks remaining, players can infer when the odds are in their favor and adjust their betting accordingly.

**Is Card Counting Legal?**

Yes, card counting is legal in most jurisdictions. It’s considered a skill-based strategy rather than cheating because it relies on mental understanding and the ability to remember which cards have been dealt rather than manipulating the game itself. However, casinos are private properties and have the right to refuse service to anyone. Therefore, if a player is suspected of card counting, casinos can ask them to leave or prohibit them from playing blackjack in their establishment. In some places, casinos employ countermeasures to make card counting less effective, such as using multiple decks of cards or shuffling frequently.

In the online context, while card counting isn’t illegal, it’s largely ineffective due to the reasons mentioned in previous responses (like RNGs and continuous shuffling).

Players must understand that while card counting can give an edge, it requires a lot of practice to be effective, and the advantage gained is typically small. Moreover, the legal and social implications of physical casinos and the technical barriers in online settings make it a challenging strategy.
