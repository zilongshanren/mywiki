## Core Rules That Shape Blackjack Probability

Blackjack is played against the dealer rather than other participants. Each player aims to achieve a hand value closer to 21 than the dealer without exceeding it. Number cards count as their face value, face cards equal 10, and aces may count as 1 or 11 depending on the situation.

The mathematical structure of the game depends heavily on its basic rules. Small variations, such as the number of decks or dealer behavior, significantly influence probability.

Typical rule components include:

- Dealer stands or hits on soft 17
- Two-card blackjack paying 3:2 or 6:5
- Ability to split pairs
- Option to double down after the initial deal

These variables determine the theoretical house edge, which usually ranges between 0.5% and 2% depending on the rules.

### Why Card Values Matter

Every blackjack decision is built around the total value of your hand compared to the dealer’s visible card. When you hold a total of 16 against a dealer’s 10, probability models suggest that standing often leads to losing outcomes.

The mathematics behind these recommendations is based on millions of simulated hands. Statistical analysis shows that specific combinations consistently produce better expected results.

### The Role of Basic Strategy Tables

Basic strategy tables translate probability calculations into practical instructions. Instead of calculating odds in real time, you follow a chart developed through statistical modeling.

Key principles often included in these charts:

- Always split aces and eights
- Avoid splitting tens
- Double down on totals of 10 or 11 when the dealer shows a weak card
- Stand on hard 17 or higher

These guidelines reduce the house edge significantly when applied consistently.

### Probability and Expected Value in Blackjack

Expected value (EV) measures the average outcome of a decision over time. In blackjack, each possible move carries a specific EV depending on the dealer’s upcard and your hand total.

For example, doubling down on a total of 11 often produces a positive expected value because the probability of receiving a 10-value card is relatively high. Conversely, hitting on a hard 17 usually lowers EV because the chance of busting exceeds the chance of improvement.
