One day after the conference part of [Mozilla Vision 2012](http://mozilla.jp/events/vision/2012/) volunteers, Mozilla staff and friends organised a “hackday” event in a burger cafe (of all places) in Tokyo. The twist: instead of catering to developer hackers [the audience was web makers](https://wiki.mozilla.org/Japan/Events/MozillaVision2012/Workshop) – and in this case those of the future:

Yes, we mostly had kids getting excited about what can be done with the web and its technologies and for this veteran of many a hack day this was an amazing experience.

The whole day was run as a series of “Popups” as defined by the [Hive learning network, NYC](http://explorecreateshare.org/2011/12/15/fireside-chat-starting-a-learning-network-in-your-city/). As explained in [Mark Surman’s blog post](http://commonspace.wordpress.com/2012/01/24/every-event-is-a-laboratory/) the activities provided were:

- Using
[Hackasaurus](http://hackasaurus.org/en-US/)to remix the web - Creating simple programs with
[MIT’s Scratch](http://scratch.mit.edu/) - Using the Paper-protyping-for-interface-design Firefox add on Domova (build by Keio University and Mozilla Japan)
- Print out a stencil and use colours to make your mark on a wall with Paint
n - Building your own platform game using the Canvas/HTML5 based
[Run Jump Build](https://github.com/jonoxia/platform-game) - Creating your own animation using Mozilla Japan’s Para Para

All of which were a big success with the kids. I especially liked the idea of Para Para, which is why I quickly interviewed Brian Birtles of Mozilla Japan about it:

All in all the second day of the event was a surprise and it showed me that there is a lot of great things to come in the next generation of web makers.

It was very refreshing to see that with some guidance and a share of enthusiasm kids happily create and use the web as a read/write medium instead of just playing games and watching movies. It is up to us to make the web of tomorrow not TV on steroids.
