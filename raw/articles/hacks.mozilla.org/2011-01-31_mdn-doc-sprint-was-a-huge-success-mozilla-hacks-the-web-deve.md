More than 30 contributors from at least a dozen countries, touching approximately 300 documentation pages.

Those are the results from the documentation sprint that started Friday, January 28 at 14:00 UTC. While the sprint was scheduled to end at midnight UTC on Saturday, some participants were still making updates on Sunday.

This sprint provided a couple of instances of cooperation with other organizations that I believe bodes well for MDN’s future as a resource, not just for Mozilla technologies, but for open web technologies in general.

**Paul Irish**, developer relations dude for Google Chrome, and one of the folks behind [Modernizr](http://www.modernizr.com/) and [HTML5 Boilerplate](http://html5boilerplate.com/), helped muster participants, and then during the sprint, added browser compatibility tables for a number of CSS and DOM pages.

**Colin Clark** and **Anastasia Cheetham** from the [Inclusive Design Research Centre](http://idrc.ocad.ca/index.php) began work on updating and migrating information on accessibility and [ARIA](http://www.w3.org/WAI/PF/aria/) from the [CodeTalks](http://wiki.codetalks.org/wiki/index.php/Main_Page) wiki to MDN. Colin first approached me about the possibility of doing this during the [Mozilla Drumbeat Festival](http://www.drumbeat.org/festival), back in November. During the sprint, Colin created [An overview of accessible web applications and widgets](https://developer.mozilla.org/en/Accessibility/An_overview_of_accessible_web_applications_and_widgets), and Anastasia created [Keyboard-navigable JavaScript widgets](https://developer.mozilla.org/en/Accessibility/Keyboard-navigable_JavaScript_widgets).

Below is a summary of the work that was accomplished. Given the massive amount of work that was done, I might have omitted something; if so, please let me know and I’ll correct this post. I am deeply grateful to everyone who contributed, and especially to those who spent large chunks of their weekend free time on the sprint.
