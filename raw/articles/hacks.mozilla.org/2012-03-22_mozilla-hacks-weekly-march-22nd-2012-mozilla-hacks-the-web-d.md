Currently Mozilla’s Developer Engagement team is having a couple of days of meetings to make sure we can continue to work as best as possible to let you know about the Open Web and what we do with Mozilla. However, that doesn’t mean there’s not time to share some good links with you!


At the end of this blog post, you also have all the [Developer Engagement team](https://wiki.mozilla.org/index.php?title=Engagement/Developer_Engagement) members and what they work on. If you are interested in discussing more, contributing or taking part of our work, don’t hesitate to contact us or follow us on Twitter!

## Weekly links

If there is anything you think we should read or know about, don’t hesitate to post a comment, contact us on Twitter or through any other means.

The picks this week are:

[HTML5 Rocks introduction to Source Maps](http://www.html5rocks.com/en/tutorials/developertools/sourcemaps/)– A way to map from compiled JavaScript back to original source files[Clickable events in CSS without :target](http://www.ryancollins.me/?p=1041)– A clever way to make clickable drop-down menu using only CSS and no Javascript[procstreams](https://github.com/polotek/procstreams)– procstreams is module to facilitate shell scripting in node. A very interesting jquery-ish take on writing shell scripts in JS.[HTTP Status Codes](http://httpstatus.es/)– Database of HTTP status codes with their IETF and Wikipedia descriptions.[SpritePad](http://spritepad.wearekiss.com/)– Create and edit spritemaps[The Return of “Best Viewed in…”](http://blog.adrianroselli.com/2012/03/return-of-viewed-in.html), on the HTML5 Please API[Hacking is Important](http://www.randsinrepose.com/archives/2012/03/13/hacking_is_important.html)– “Hackers believe that something can always be better, and that nothing is ever complete.” Right?[Roundup of HTML-Based Slide Deck Toolkits](http://www.impressivewebs.com/html-slidedeck-toolkits/)[Control a massive modular synth at MIT](http://synth.media.mit.edu/synthclient/)– from the web!
