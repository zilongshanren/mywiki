A couple days ago Michael Lugo at [God Plays Dice](http://godplaysdice.blogspot.com/2010/04/mathematical-relationships-search.html) shared a link to a [mathematical relationships search](http://www.thatsmathematics.com/relation.php). Enter the names of two people with PhDs in mathematics and it will spit out their academic relationship. For example, my advisor, [John Franks](http://hopf.math.northwestern.edu/~j-franks/), is my academic father and my good friend and collaborator [Jim Wiseman](http://ecademy.agnesscott.edu/~jwiseman/) is my academic brother. It draws the information from the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](http://www.genealogy.ams.org/).

It is fun to play with. For example, Euler is my great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-grandfather and Erdős is my tenth cousin four times removed. By the way, I think I finally understand what it means to have [nth cousin m times removed](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin).

Interestingly when I look at my relationship to different mathematicians, it is often the case that our common ancestor is Simeon Poisson who had only three advisees: Chasles, Dirichlet, and Louiville.

There is another interesting website that performs a similar function. While the mathematical relationships search pulls information from the genealogical tree, the [AMS runs a website](http://www.ams.org/mathscinet/collaborationDistance.html) that pulls information from the graph of collaborators (these data come from [MathSciNet](http://www.ams.org/mathscinet/search.html)).

The [collaboration distance](http://www.ams.org/mathscinet/collaborationDistance.html) generalizes the well known [Erdős number](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_number). My Erdős number is 4; that means that I wrote a paper with someone who wrote a paper with someone who wrote a paper with someone who wrote a paper with Erdős. Similarly, my collaboration number with John Forbes Nash, Jr., Grisha Perelman, Steven Strogatz, and Terence Tao is 5 and my collaboration number with John H. Conway is 4.
