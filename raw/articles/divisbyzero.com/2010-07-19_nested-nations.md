Recently I [wrote about](https://divisbyzero.com/2010/07/12/water-and-land-nested/) a neat land/water recursion that I found. I’ve seen countries that are completely surrounded by other countries (called [enclaves](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave_and_exclave))—[Lesotho](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho) and [San Marino](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino) being two examples. But I had no idea just how recursive this could become.

Michael Tobis left a [comment](https://divisbyzero.com/2010/07/12/water-and-land-nested/#comment-1392) with a link to an eye-opening post on his [Pining for Fjords](http://pining.blogspot.com/2010/07/enclave-within-enclave-within-enclave.html) blog. He points out that there is a piece of India within Bangladesh within India within Bangladesh—a third-order enclave! The map below was posted with fascinating commentary on the [Strange Maps](http://strangemaps.wordpress.com/2007/05/08/110-cooch-behar-the-mother-of-all-enclave-complexes/) blog a few years ago. It shows that area 51 (India) sits inside area 19 (Bangladesh—East Pakistan at that time the map was made) which sits inside area 47 (India) which is in Bangladesh. Wow! For more information see this [document](http://eprints.unimelb.edu.au/archive/00001443/) by Brendan Whyte.

I was hoping that I could see these enclaves in Google Maps, but they aren’t shown (see below). However, I was struck by the fractal-like appearance of the border between India and Bangladesh.

While not as recursive as the India/Bangladesh enclaves, I also discovered second-order enclaves in the Netherlands. Inside the Dutch municipality of [Baarle-Nassau](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarle-Nassau) is the Belgian enclave called [Baarle-Hertog](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarle-Hertog) which consists of 24 parcels of land, and within it are 7 Dutch enclaves.

Excellent! I love the way this nesting approach connects micronations to the very large world.

Sounds like a fun teaching tool, too.
