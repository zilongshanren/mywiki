Every day for lunch I eat salad (made with vegetables from our [local farmers’ market](http://www.farmersonthesquare.com/) or from our [college’s organic farm](http://www.dickinson.edu/about/sustainability/college-farm/)) and [homemade yogurt](http://www.nytimes.com/2009/04/15/dining/15curi.html) and [granola](http://www.slashfood.com/2007/08/31/a-granola-recipe-from-my-moms-hippie-youth/). The only time I ever eat fast food is on long car trips. So why, I ask you, did the question “What shape are the golden arches?” pop into my head?

I have no idea. But once it did, I just had to investigate. A quick internet search was inconclusive. Commenters on discussion forums assert that they are a pair of [parabolas](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabola) or a pair of [catenary curves](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenary). But the credibility of the sources is questionable. So I thought I’d see what I could determine using Geogebra.

It turns out that the arches are definitely not parabolas (I didn’t think they were). The catenary is a good fit, but it still isn’t quite perfect. The best fit is an ellipse (or part of an ellipse)! Check out [the applet](http://users.dickinson.edu/~richesod/golden/arches.html) that I made, and see for yourself.
