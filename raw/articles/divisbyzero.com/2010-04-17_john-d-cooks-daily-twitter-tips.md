One of the most prolific and interesting math bloggers and Twitters, John D. Cook (his blog is [The Endeavor](http://www.johndcook.com/blog/) and he is [@JohnDCook](http://twitter.com/johndcook) on Twitter) has been using Twitter in a very interesting way. In addition to his personal account, he has set up more than half a dozen daily Twitter messages on a variety of topics. (Apparently he composes most of the tweets in advance and has them posted automatically once a day, 7 days a week.)

Here are his daily mathematical Twitter accounts:

[@AlgebraFact](http://twitter.com/AlgebraFact): a mixture of linear algebra, number theory, group theory, etc.


[Example]: If 2^p – 1 is prime, 2^{p-1}(2^p – 1) is an even perfect number. Euclid, Prob IX.36.

[@ProbFact](http://twitter.com/ProbFact): theorems in probability, plus historical tidbits and applications.


[Example]: The mean of a Poisson random variable equals its variance. This can be a problem in applications.[http://bit.ly/ijNfA]

[@AnalysisFact](http://twitter.com/AnalysisFact): facts from real and complex analysis (ranging from elementary to advanced).


[Example]: Complex conjugate maps x + iy to x – iy. This is not an analytic function.

[@TopologyFact](http://twitter.com/TopologyFact): Facts from topology and geometry


[Example]: The image of a compact set under a continuous function is compact.

[@TeXtip](http://twitter.com/TeXtip): Tips for learning TeX and LaTeX (coming soon)

(He also has two other daily accounts: [@RegexTip](http://twitter.com/RegexTip) on regular expressions and [@SansMouse](http://twitter.com/SansMouse) on keyboard shortcuts in Windows.)
