In my multivariable calculus we’re talking about parametric curves.

I’m using [this applet](http://users.dickinson.edu/~richesod/math261/parametric.html) for displaying parametric curves. You can use predefined curves or enter your own. Although the applet is on my web page, it was created by [Marc Renault](http://webspace.ship.edu/msrenault/), a friend who teaches down the road at Shippensburg University. I only tweaked it slightly when I posted it on my page.

For fun, I created [this applet](http://www.geogebratube.org/student/m158134). It has four parametric curves which, when drawn together, produce a [famous logo](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers). Our college is in Pennsylvania, so it went over well.

Marc also created this nice [applet illustrating the creation of the cycloid](http://webspace.ship.edu/msrenault/ggb/cycloid.html).

Speaking of the cycloid, after the deriving the parametric equations for the cycloid I spent 10 minutes telling my class about the tautochrone and brachistochrone problems.

They **loved** the story about [the history of the brachistochrone problem](http://www-history.mcs.st-and.ac.uk/HistTopics/Brachistochrone.html)—the way that Johann Bernoulli taunted Newton, Newton’s 12-hour after-work solution, Newton’s annoyance (“I do not love to be dunned [pestered] and teased by foreigners about mathematical things…”), his anonymous solution, and Bernoulli’s reply (“I recognize the lion by his paw.”).

This afternoon, after teaching the class, I [discovered](http://mathworld.wolfram.com/TautochroneProblem.html) that the tautochrone problem is mentioned in *Moby Dick*: “[The try-pot] is also a place for profound mathematical meditation. It was in the left-hand try-pot of the *Pequod*, with the soapstone diligently circling round me, that I was first indirectly struck by the remarkable fact, that in geometry all bodies gliding along a cycloid, my soapstone, for example, will descend from any point in precisely the same time.” Cool!
