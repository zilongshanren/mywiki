The arguments around the use of "AI" are exhausting. I'm tired of re-typing my reasons, so this document is a summary of some of the many reasons why **I refuse to use AI**.

- These reasons are not particularly original. They have been stated many times by others.
- These are not
*all*the reasons not to use AI. - These reasons are not in any particular order.
- These arguments focus on my work as a programmer.
- I interchange AI and LLMs, but prefer LLMs as "AI" is a marketing term used to oversell the capability of generative models.

## Deskilling

When workers become reliant on LLMs to help them with their work, they turn **from unique individuals with unique sets of skills, to generalist workers** who only know how to enter prompts.

This makes the workers **much easier to replace**.

This is extremely useful for large corporations looking to reduce one of the largest costs in running a business; **wages**.

I do not want to be a replaceable prompt-writer.

In March 2026 the CEO of OpenAI, Sam Altman confirmed that one of their goals is to make users reliant on them for intelligence.

The whole "AI" industry is just based on the hope that they can

deskill peoplefast enough that they'll have torent back cognitive support systems. Forever.

## Built on Stolen Labour

Large language models are trained on work created by humans. Without human-created training data, they are useless. Companies create models trained on human-made work and then sell it back to the people that created it. The creators of models have openly admitted that their product [would not work if they had to pay for the work that they steal](https://www.salon.com/2024/01/09/impossible-openai-admits-chatgpt-cant-exist-without-pinching-copyrighted-work/).

This is morally wrong.

## Negative Environmental Impacts

This has been well-documented but to restate the obvious; training and using LLMs is an **order of magnitude more energy-demanding** than traditional computing. The UN estimates that a ChatGPT query requires [10 times the electricity of a traditional search](https://unric.org/en/artificial-intelligence-how-much-energy-does-ai-use/). Creating a 5-second video is [like running a microwave for an hour](https://mashable.com/article/energy-ai-worse-than-we-thought). We are currently [on track to hit catastrophic 3C increases](https://www.politico.eu/article/united-nations-emissions-gap-global-warming-data-climate-change-report/). We should be using *less* electricity, not *more*.

In the same way that [crypto uses more electricity than some countries](https://www.cnet.com/home/energy-and-utilities/how-much-power-does-crypto-use-the-government-wants-to-know/), AI companies are wasting so much that they are [turning to portable methane gas generators](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/05/06/elon-musk-xai-memphis-gas-turbines-air-pollution-permits-00317582) for power.

And no, AI will not save us from climate change.

## Introduces Reliance on Subscription Service

This seems minor, but becoming reliant on a subscription service in order to do my work seems like an extremely bad idea. What happens when the service goes down? Or becomes more expensive? Or the [quality goes down](https://community.openai.com/t/has-there-been-a-recent-decrease-in-gpt-4-quality/207392)?

## I Like Programming

When I try to explain programming to children, I like to say **programming is like building with LEGO**. You come up with something you want to make, and using some standard bits you work out how to do it.

**With LLMs, you don't get to play with the LEGO**. An algorithm gets to play with the LEGO and you just get to look at the results. The LLM has all the fun, and you're relegated to saying "no, make it more like this".

That's not fun.

## Fundamentally Hostile to Human Learning

Some people use LLMs to solve problems that are out of their area of expertise. I know researchers who use it to write Python scripts to visualise their data. Their goal is to continue with their research, so they don't want to have to spend time learning the nuances of whatever Python scripting library they want to use.

Using LLMs stops you from learning those skills. Students who use LLMs like ChatGPT [are unable to write essays or even understand text without help](https://archive.is/20250514075926/https://www.chronicle.com/article/is-ai-enhancing-education-or-replacing-it). A study found that LLM users [couldn't remember what they wrote](https://techstartups.com/2025/06/19/mit-study-finds-that-chatgpt-is-making-people-dumber-83-of-chatgpt-users-cant-recall-what-they-just-wrote/) and had far worse learning outcomes than people who did it themselves or used a search engine.

This trend in society is extremely dangerous. It promotes [using tools without understanding them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_cult_programming). As late-stage capitalism continues to try to extract more value from workers, it forces them to work faster, at the expense of long-term improvements. Going slower and **learning how to do something yourself pays off for you in the long term**.

## Worse Quality Code

Using LLMs to generate code leads to [worse quality code](https://visualstudiomagazine.com/Articles/2024/01/25/copilot-research.aspx). It creates code that is more brittle, uses less existing code, and is harder to debug because it was not written by anybody on the project.

It even leads to [writing code slower](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), despite it **feeling** faster.

There are already examples of Microsoft engineers [publicly trying (and failing)](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/pull/115733) to get Copilot to write sensible PRs.

Using LLMs to generate code is effectively a hyper-charged version of copy-pasting code from the internet directly into your software.

## It's a Black Box

When an LLM generates an incorrect answer or does something unexpected, there is almost nothing you can do as a user to fix the model so it does not happen again. You can try to correct it in the short term by adding more to your prompt, but long-term you are dealing with a black box of probabilities.

## Inaccuracy and Bad User Experience

The experience of using an LLM to solve a problem is [very similar to using voice-control like Alexa or Siri](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=maAFcEU6atk). You ask it to do something, and when it does the wrong thing, you're left explaining yourself in greater and greater detail until you might as well have solved the problem yourself.

When used for generating code, it reminds me of the the [peanut butter programming challenge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=58QePlerInM). As a user you are using inaccurate, vague English to try to create precise code that defines behaviour. So inaccuracies happen, and you need to fix the code.

There is already a way to precisely define the behaviour of a program.

It's called programming.

## Fundamentally Designed to Make Stuff Up

LLMs are essentially **hyper-charged versions of autocomplete**; based on a prompt, they output the **most statistically-likely text**, there is no concept of what is *correct*, only what is *likely*.

Trying to massage the results by adding "only say truthful stuff" to the prompt is futile. The model has no concept of truth.

## The Statistical Average is the Least Interesting Result

LLMs at their core produce statistical averages. Given a set of training data, the model it produces can be used to output the most common words and phrases given an input.

The most common words and phrases are **over-used, trite, cliché**. Something produced by a human has the capacity to be unexpected and creative in a way that an LLM never could:

The ships hung in the sky in much the same way that bricks don't. Douglas Adams, The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy

## Inability to Solve Novel Problems

Imagine that a new programming language or piece of software is released. If you ask an LLM how to write for it, the LLM will be incredibly inaccurate as it does not know about that technology.

Until a human writes documentation and tutorials, and the LLM steals that data, the LLM is useless.

[Using "AI" to track and kill people](https://www.jpost.com/israel-news/defense-news/article-851549). If civilians are killed, it is discounted as a "bug" with no human held accountable.- "Full self-driving" is a feature that companies like Tesla use to sell their cars. However when

## LLMs Ruin the Web

My first programming language was PHP. I had a website in the early 2000s. **I love the web.** Specifically, I love the [small web](https://ar.al/2020/08/07/what-is-the-small-web/); sites made by amateurs with a passion for something. They are always the most interesting, the most informative and the most *fun*.

LLMs ruined that. The web was already being killed by Google and SEO, but LLMs made it so most sites are filled with meaningless walls of text. Text that is *plausible* but not true in its meaning.

## Non-deterministic

Imagine someone pitches you a new kind of calculator. This new tool **gives you a different answer from one day to the next**.

Companies are replacing deterministic hand-made systems with ones based on LLMs. The tools may appear the same on the surface, but underneath they use non-deterministic LLMs whose answer could change from one day to the next, for any number of reasons:

- The company providing the service could update the model, changing the results.
- The system could take into account previous prompts, changing what is produced based on previous input.
- The LLM could be built with a certain amount of randomness in its responses (aka "temperature").

## Not Using Your Brain is Insulting

If you won't spend the time to write me an email, why should I spend the time to read it? You might as well just send me the prompt and save yourself the subscription and save the planet the huge CO2 hit.

When people use LLMs, we end up in the ridiculous situation where senders use a few words to generate a wall of text, and then the recipient uses an LLM to summarize the email back into a few words. All with the potential for hallucination, miscommunication, massive energy waste, etc.

Long-term interaction with chat-bot style LLMs is causing [psychosis](https://archive.is/piKSW) in a large number of users.

People are falling in love with their chat-bots.

I have enough problems as it is.

## LLMs Make Everyone Poorer

Let's say I pay a local artist $100 to make a picture. They in turn take that $100, and use $20 for lunch, $10 for coffees, $50 on groceries, etc. That original $100 goes around local stores and enriches everyone a little bit.

If instead, I pay $10 a month for a service that makes AI slop pictures, where does that money go? It goes to a billionaire getting another island. It goes for electricity costs on a datacentre that employs only a few people.

Using "AI" makes regular people poorer, and concentrates wealth and power in the hands of the ultra-rich. I don't want to be a part of that.

## Something made with no effort has no value

Imagine your friend made a cup for you.

Imagine they made it completely from scratch. They dug up the clay. They made a potter's wheel to shape the cup. They researched local kilns and made friends with the people that work there. They fired the cup. They painted and glazed the cup. They fired it for a second time. They gave it to you.

Now imagine an alternative scenario. Your friend instead pressed a "Make Cup" button, and out popped a cup.

Imagine the two cups look identical. There is no way to tell them apart.

From a functional and visual standpoint, the two cups are the same. However their **provenance** is completely different.

People care about the provenance of items. I think the majority of people would say **the cup created through greater effort has more value than the one created by pressing a button**.

If people find out that an essay is created by an AI, they [give it a lower rating than if they think it is made by a human](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749597825000172).

This is not humans being illogical, this is humans understanding that in a finite lifetime, spending time on a thing gives it more value.
