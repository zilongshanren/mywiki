If you’re a Japanese speaker, you can now read my book, *Mastering Emacs*, in Japanese. I owe all this hard work to [AYANOKOJI Takesi](https://github.com/ayatakesi) and [USAMI Kenta](https://www.zonu.me), two legendary Emacs hackers and writers in the Japanese Emacs community. There’s a large Emacs community in Japan but, unless you speak Japanese, you probably wouldn’t know that!

It’s easy to miss, but Emacs owes its superb Unicode and multilingual support to the Japanese [National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Advanced_Industrial_Science_and_Technology) who created (and maintained) a separate fork of Emacs called *Nihongo (“Japanese”) Emacs*, first created in 1987. Later it was wrapped up into a separate package called `MULE`

(`M-x find-library mule`

.)

`MULE`

was merged into Emacs 20.1 in 1997 (`C-u 20 C-h n`

to open the `NEWS`

file from then and search for `MULE`

), and it’s one of the few [exceptions Richard Stallman and the FSF](https://mail.gnu.org/archive/html/bug-gnu-emacs/2000-09/msg00065.html) made to their ironclad rule that copyright must be assigned to the FSF. So there’s a bit of trivia for you.

So, once again, thanks to Takesi and Kenta for their hard work. If you own my book, you can download the translated version for free.
