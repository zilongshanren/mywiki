**tools I use on my iPad, on my Samsung Galaxy (Android) and on the cloud**...[Dropbox](https://www.dropbox.com/). Easy and really really important to me. I use it both for photography and coding, it's available on Mac, Pc, Android, iOS and Web, so it covers everything. I particularly love the Android integration which allows me to snap photos of notes and things I want to remember and then upload them directly into my dropbox account. Essential![Wunderlist](http://www.6wunderkinder.com/wunderlist/). Another cross-everything tool, it replaced the non-cloud tools I had on iOS- I prefer it to Remember The Milk because even if the latter is probably more powerful, it's free account it too restricted for me, and the paid one a bit too expensive.
- Many people swear by Evernote. Maybe one day, I like a lot keeping most of my stuff on Dropbox today...
- ...speaking of which
[PlainText](http://itunes.apple.com/ca/app/plaintext-dropbox-text-editing/id391254385?mt=8)on iOS is a neat text-editor that syncs into a folder in your dropbox account. There are a number of similar editors nowadays, even with Markdown functionalities, I don't end up using it very often anyways. - For handwriting
[Bamboo Paper](http://itunes.apple.com/us/app/bamboo-paper-notebook/id443131313?mt=8). Best natural writing app I've found, I think it feels better than Penultimate which is very good too.[Paper](http://www.fiftythree.com/paper)is nice to make diagrams and sketches look pretty easily. - One day I'll buy a
[Jot Touch](http://adonit.net/jot/touch/), I like the Jot pens, but if you use an antiglare screen (as I used to,[PowerSupport](http://www.powersupportusa.com/)seems to be the best) then it might scratch it... [Reeder](http://reederapp.com/)for iOS was my choice for google reader offline reading, but now google reader is dead and the iPad version of Reeder seems dead too. My solution:[MrReader](http://www.curioustimes.de/mrreader/)and[Feedly](http://cloud.feedly.com/#welcome)[iAnnotate](http://www.branchfire.com/iannotate/)PDF. Best PDF reader for iOS that I've found- VLC (videolan) player for iOS. Has been pulled from the store over a petty argument about its licensing, but now it's back!
[ReadItLater](http://readitlaterlist.com/)(now called Pocket), both the iOS client and I have bookmarklets on my various browsers. I use it also as an offline reading tool, especially when I travel I upload all the travel guides I want (i.e. wikitravel) and then cache them on the iOS client...[ZooTool](http://zootool.com/everyone)for bookmarks (which for me is bookmark sharing, I don't really care about the bookmark library myself, I just figured that I do a lot of work to read feeds and other stuff on the web so I can create and share such a list for others to benefit...). I'm shying away from this as I use Twitter more and more- Just started trying
[Spreed](http://spreeder.com/), which is a cool web based speed reader with a nice bookmarklet. - iTunesU/Coursera/and similar
[http://www.rainymood.com/](http://www.rainymood.com/)rarely, as I don't mind the noise and I often find background chatter to be quite nice, I listen to a lot of Italian news and politics at work :)[http://sleepyti.me/](http://sleepyti.me/)

Other stuff that I use that are not really work related:

**iOS**: Zinio (magazine newsstand), Fancy (cool stuff), SpyderGallery (as I have a Spyder color calibrator), Air Video (great to share videos from my iMac to the iPad), Photosmith (at a given point I wanted to write something exactly like that, to allow selection and rating which is a tedious process, of Lightroom photo collections), Daytum (personal logging)**Android**: WhatsApp (messaging), Glympse (rarely used) and Poynt (rarely used...), HDR Camera (half-decent, the HDR merging is good but the alignment is really cheap and looses sharpness)**Cloud**: Yelp, to decide where/what to eat, PreyProject.com to protect my macbook and my Android stuff, LogMeIn for remoting**Physical world**: I write on paper. Yes, I've tried iPad and styluses and gloves and palm rejection and everything. It's terrible, and thinking otherwise is just a delusion caused by the fact we love our gadgets. It's many orders of magnitudes worse. And if you need a digital copy, just take a picture of the page with a cellphone. Now, that said, which pen and which paper, that's an interesting thing.- An A5-A6 spiral bound notebook. It's important to be spiral bound with an hardcover, if you're writing while commuting and so on you need a hard surface, the spiral bound books allow only the page you're writing on to be facing you and with the cover on the back they are the best in terms of stability.
[Field notes](http://officesupplygeek.com/notebook-review/spiral-binding/field-notes-brand-steno-pad/),[Whitelines](http://whitelines.se/products/category/wire-top/?item=wl-83)or the classic[Rhodia](http://rhodiapads.com/). - Pencil, I use a
[KuruToga](http://officesupplygeek.com/pencil-review/uniball-kuru-toga-pencil-review-and-big-giveaway/)because it's cool with[Uniball NanoDia](http://officesupplygeek.com/pen-review/pencils/uni-ball-nanodia-0-5-mm-2h-low-wear-pencil-lead/)leads (fairly soft). - Pens. Too many, I collect fountain ones and buy too many other writing instruments in general, from brush pens to very fine ballpoints to graphite and brushes... For an everyday fountain surely the best choice is a
[Lamy Safari](http://www.lamy.com/eng/b2b/safari/018). You want to couple it with a very smooth flowing ink like the[Aurora black](http://www.marcuslink.com/pens/ink/aurora.htm).
