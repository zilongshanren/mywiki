I know that there are many more programming languages and tools, and I'm sure I've blogged about some of those already in various occasions, but I think I covered the best ones in this update.

[Kangaroo](http://groups.google.co.uk/group/kangaroophysics): a physics system for Grasshopper...

[Structure Synth](http://structuresynth.sourceforge.net/): rule-based generative 3d shapes

[Groboto](http://www.braid.com/groboto-site/): as made famous by the poster of the last Siggraph conference.

[Houdini](http://www.sidefx.com/): commercial 3d DCC application, very strong in its procedural components. Free trial.

[Softimage ICE](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodesk_Softimage#ICE_Interactive_Creative_Environment): Softimage has a very powerful visual scripting language called ICE, which is extensively used to do generative art. Free trial.

Other companies in the the industrial design/architecture realm offer generative solutions. Some examples are Bentley's

[GenerativeComponents](http://www.bentley.com/en-US/Products/GenerativeComponents/)and

[ParaCould](http://paraclouding.com/home/)tools.

[This conference](http://smartgeometry.org/)is pretty cool.

[Realflow](http://www.realflow.com/): commercial fluids and physics solution. A popular free alternative (that I hate due to its retarded interface) is

[Blender](http://www.blender.org/), with its physics and fluid systems.

[TopMod](http://code.google.com/p/topmod/): topological modeller, opensource

[Shapeways](http://www.shapeways.com/): affordable 3d printing on a lot of different materials

[RepRap](http://reprap.org/wiki/Main_Page),

[MakerBot](http://www.makerbot.com/),

[Fab@Home](http://fabathome.org/): cheap DIY 3d printers

**- Procedural/Generative/Interactive Art Languages -**


*examples*


[Proce55ing](http://processing.org/): from MIT aesthetic & computation group, it's a very popular java-like language, with lots of

[extension](http://toxiclibs.org/)

[libraries](http://processing.org/reference/libraries/index.html). Very simple, I use this for prototyping a lot. There are also "ports" in other languages, one of the coolest if the

[Clojure](http://github.com/rosado/clj-processing)one (because Clojure is ubercool).

[vvvv](http://vvvv.org/tiki-index.php): visual programming, you connect blocks. Graphics oriented, DX-only and fairly cool. A similar tool but newer and as of now, less popular, is Derivative's

[TouchDesigner](http://www.touch077.com/Products/). Someone more demoscenically inclined may want to use

[werkkzeug](http://www.theprodukkt.com/)too, even if it's quite old now.

[Field](http://openendedgroup.com:8000/field/wiki/OverviewBanners2): very cool python and java mix, realtime programming environment. A bit young but I hope it will become stronger and stronger. OSX only.

[Scriptographer](http://scriptographer.org/about/): a scripting plugin for adobe Illustrator. It's also the software that drives

[Hektor](http://www.scratchdisk.com/Work/Hektor/), by the same author, which it fairly cool.

**- Libraries**

[Cinder](http://libcinder.org/): a library developed by the excellent

[Barbarian group](http://www.barbariangroup.com/). Claims to be "professional grade". Works on iOs platforms as well. Abstracts the usual stuff, graphics, IO and so on.

[openFrameworks](http://www.openframeworks.cc/): very similar to Cinder, very popular as well, a bit older. Also works on the various iOs platforms.

[CGAL](http://www.cgal.org/): computational geometry. Not made for generative art, but surely handy, there is also a toolbox that integrates it into

[scilab](http://cglab.gforge.inria.fr/). Very comprehensive. Other nice ones are

[OpenMesh,](http://www.openmesh.org/)

[MeshLab](http://meshlab.sourceforge.net/),

[VCGLib](http://vcg.sourceforge.net/index.php/Main_Page),

[GeometricTools](http://www.geometrictools.com/index.html)and

[GTS](http://gts.sourceforge.net/).

[Physic](http://bulletphysics.org/wordpress/)

[simulation](http://www.box2d.org/)libraries are also commonly used, but really the possibilities are endless.

[OpenCV](http://sourceforge.net/projects/opencvlibrary/): the standard Computer Vision library, very popular, there are wrappers for a lot of different languages and tools.

Offline renderers are also among the tools of the trade. There are

[so many](http://www.pointzero.nl/renderers/)out there, but probably the most interesting for this trading are either the photorealistic path tracers like

[luxRender](http://www.luxrender.net/)that have the virtue of letting you focus on the shapes you're emitting, or the programmable ones, renderman clones like

[Aqsis](http://www.aqsis.org/)and

[Pixie](http://www.renderpixie.com/)or raytracers like

[SunFlow](http://sunflow.sourceforge.net/). All those that I listed are opensource too, an added virtue for the generative artist.

**- Data banks -**


*examples*


Data visualization can be an art, can be profitable, and can even try to

[tell some truths](http://www.ted.com/talks/hans_rosling_shows_the_best_stats_you_ve_ever_seen.html). But free data access is not so common as you might think. Some useful resources are:

[WorldBank](http://data.worldbank.org/),

[Data.gov](http://data.gov/),

[Google Public Data](http://www.google.com/publicdata/home),

[UNData](http://data.un.org/),

[GapMinder](http://www.gapminder.org/).

Wolfram Mathematica, not only is a very nice CAS with a lisp-like language, but it also comes with access to a wealth of different

[data sources](http://www.wolfram.com/products/mathematica/analysis/content/ScientificDataSources.html).
