An exoplanet is a planet that orbits another star other than the Sun; since 1988, approximately 2000 of them have been confirmed. This post, inspired by [Ethan Kruse](https://twitter.com/ethan_kruse)‘s [Kepler Orrery IV](http://imgur.com/tdOWaWv), visualises and animates exoplanets, together with their habitable zones. The data comes from the [NASA Exoplanet Archive](http://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/) and it only includes the confirmed exoplanets with known orbits and temperature. You can find a complete list of all discovered exoplanets since January 2021 in the [Exoplanet Catalogue](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=3986).

### Habitable zones

The green area indicates [circumstellar habitable zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumstellar_habitable_zone), regions around stars that receive enough light to allow for liquid water to form. For each orrery, the inner and outer bounds of each habitable zone are defined as ![Rendered by QuickLaTeX.com \sqrt{L/1.1}](../../assets/87f92910037de94a.png)

![Rendered by QuickLaTeX.com \sqrt{L/0.53}](../../assets/05dcd1cef69fc226.png)

![Rendered by QuickLaTeX.com L](../../assets/6046f3c7b4bcbd21.png)


Being within the habitable zones is not a guarantee of liquid water. Atmospheric pressure, surface albedo, tidal forces and radiations can all affect the temperature of a planet. Exoplanet temperatures are not measured directly; we refer to [planetary equilibrium temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_equilibrium_temperature) instead, which takes into account only the amount of light received from the nearby star. ### Large systems

The majority of these planets have been discovered by [transit photometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_detecting_exoplanets#Transit_photometry); a technique which detects the drop in luminosity that a star suffers when is eclipsed by one of its planets. Monitoring starts for long period is expensive, with the consequence that the majority of the exoplanets discovered have very fast orbits. Their orbital speed is to scale (![Rendered by QuickLaTeX.com 1 second = 3 day](../../assets/ec2ff4cb80beb13f.png)


In these orreries, stars are always assumed to be in the right focus of the elliptical orbits. Planetary initial positions are synchronised so that when they hit zero degrees, they transit in front of their stars, from the Earth’s perspective.
